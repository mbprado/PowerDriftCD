# 2026-10-20 perspective-road ASIC prototype

**Question / hypothesis:** can the validated Sega CD graphics ASIC and VDP path display a deterministic 64×64 road surface whose scanline traces vary with depth, curvature, an elevation cue, and camera bank, at 30 updates/s in BlastEm? **Expected acceptance:** 60 checked ASIC outputs, a visibly changing road, 59 successive two-VBlank frame intervals, no VBlank upload misses, and a U.S. Model 1 compatibility checkpoint on the unchanged image. **Result — verified by execution:** `PASS` in three U.S. Model 2 BlastEm boots and one Model 1 checkpoint. This is an L1 emulator result, not a physical-hardware result or a production renderer.

## Scene and transfer path

**Verified by execution:** `tools/make_perspective_road.py` creates two original 16×16 four-bit stamps: a striped grass stamp and a gray road stamp with white edges, red shoulders, and a dashed yellow center. A 16×16 stamp map places the road in one source-map column and grass elsewhere. For each of 24 camera states, it generates 64 ASIC trace lines. The horizontal source step decreases from two to one-half source pixel per output pixel toward the viewer, making the road widen with depth. Per-line X starts bend the road and include a bank-dependent shift; per-pixel Y deltas shear its markings. A changing horizon and nonlinear source-Y progression give an elevation/pitch *cue*. The 24 states repeat across 60 updates; the host generates the trace vectors before boot, while the Sega CD ASIC performs every frame's actual pixel transformation at runtime. This distinction matters when interpreting CPU cost.

**Verified by execution:** the sub-CPU copies the chosen 512-byte trace table into 2M Word RAM, configures the ASIC, waits for its busy bit to clear, and returns Word RAM. The main CPU checks a generated 32-bit sum over all 1,024 output words before staging 2,048 bytes in main RAM during active display. It then uploads those bytes to VDP VRAM by DMA at VBlank. The 64 VDP tiles occupy a 64×64 area on plane A. The one-frame skip intentionally targets 30 updates/s. This reuses the already validated registers, memory layout, transfer mechanism, and stopwatch windows from [Phase 1](graphics-characterization.md); Sega's [Mega-CD software manual](https://segaretro.org/images/archive/6/6f/20190509144929%21Mega-CD_Software_Development_Manual.pdf) and [Genesis software manual](https://segaretro.org/images/9/95/GenesisSoftwareManual.pdf) remain the register references. No undocumented ASIC behavior was assumed.

**Verified by execution:** all three Model 2 boots reached `GVOK` (`D3=$47564F4B`) with `D6=$003C` and no checksum failure. The [early screenshot](data/perspective-road-early.png) and [final screenshot](data/perspective-road-final.png) differ, showing the displayed road changes as the deterministic camera state advances. Their SHA-256 values are `34f06c791c436c8d978fa985f32b333d79cae7bd09eb0efb3a0a240096a940ac` and `0d1b5bb4ec14d3b5fa051a2662e5f20b655197365617779deed7b3244a0196e2`. The 60 generated expected output sums contain 24 distinct values. Checksums plus screenshots support output correctness but do not constitute a word-for-word dump of every ASIC frame.

**Verified by execution:** the 60 parsed frame records were identical across all three boots, including their per-frame timing observations. The ASIC interval includes its trigger write, polling, and timer read; the composite interval includes Word RAM arbitration, stamp/map/trace copying, output clearing, ASIC work, and return. Staging and DMA are separate measured windows. These are neither isolated ASIC cycles nor a full game-frame budget. The maximum individual recorded interval is below the 4,096-tick timer wrap, but the stopwatch cannot prove that an unobserved full lap never occurred during a long stall. The earlier Phase 1 timer controls and bounded operation sizes make such a stall unlikely for these observed runs.

Its final screenshot was byte-identical to Model 2's final screenshot (SHA-256 `0d1b5bb4ec14d3b5fa051a2662e5f20b655197365617779deed7b3244a0196e2`). This was a compatibility checkpoint, not a fourth complete timing run. Model 2 remains the routine baseline.

## Limits and next test

**Measured in BlastEm:** this scene sustains the intended 64×64/30 Hz path with no observed upload deadline misses. **Hypothesis:** the Phase 1 recommendation to pre-stage and DMA 2 KiB remains sound for a small road viewport; this prototype adds only modest composite work relative to the Phase 1 64×64 fixture. The result does not establish a final rendering architecture or stock-hardware speed.

**Unsupported / not tested:** the 24 scanline trace sets are generated offline, so the sub-CPU cost of computing arbitrary camera transforms at runtime is unmeasured. The elevation effect is a source-Y/horizon warp, not a heightfield with correct occlusion or a physically modeled crest. The scene has one road strip, repeating grass, no scenery, sprites, double buffering, road turns beyond its finite camera sequence, 128×64 stress case, or complete 60 Hz road test. An additive word sum could miss compensating pixel errors; exact output bytes for all 60 frames were not dumped. No independent emulator or physical hardware was used; the original-only boot issue is still open.

**Next controlled experiment — hypothesis:** compute the 64 trace lines from compact camera parameters on the sub-CPU at runtime, keeping the same texture, viewport, checksums, and transfer path. Measure trace calculation separately from ASIC completion and compare it with this precomputed-trace baseline. Only then stress 128×64 and richer road/scenery composition. Do not infer a complete Power Drift renderer from this synthetic scene.

**2026-10-20 follow-up — verified by execution:** the [runtime-trace experiment](2026-10-20-runtime-road-traces.md) built depth/scale/source-step rows on the sub-CPU from compact, partly precomputed camera data. A two-pass checksum/staging path dropped to 20 Hz; combining the passes restored 30 Hz over three Model 2 boots, with a matching Model 1 final screenshot. Fully calculated, arbitrary camera parameters remain an open test.
