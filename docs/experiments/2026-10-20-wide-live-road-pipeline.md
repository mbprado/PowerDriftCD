# 2026-10-20 128×64 live-command road pipeline

**Question / hypothesis:** does a 128×64 ASIC output, with the same five-word camera-command interface and VDP transfer path, sustain 30 updates/s in BlastEm? **Baseline — verified by execution:** the [64×64 live-command image](2026-10-20-live-road-camera-commands.md) passed three 60-update Model 2 runs at 30 Hz; its worst image-ready time was 510 emulated stopwatch ticks after the previous VBlank. **Expected evidence:** 60 checked wide outputs, 59 two-VBlank intervals of 1,085–1,088 ticks, no upload misses, repeated cold boots, and a Model 1 checkpoint.

**Result — verified by execution:** the first 128×64 image produced all 60 correct checked outputs but displayed them every *three* VBlanks, about 20 Hz, because the existing wait loop skipped a VBlank even when the next image was already ready before the intended 30 Hz upload. A conditional wait based on measured elapsed stopwatch ticks restored two-VBlank cadence. Three independent U.S. Model 2 boots of the corrected image produced identical 60-frame records, 59 intervals of 1,086–1,087 ticks, and zero cadence or VBlank upload misses. The identical disc reached `GVOK` and update 60 under the owner-labelled U.S. Model 1 BIOS. These are L1 BlastEm observations; no physical Sega CD was tested.

## Scene, transfer and scheduling

**Documented only:** Sega's [Mega-CD Hardware Manual](https://segaretro.org/images/5/51/Mega-CD_Hardware_Manual.pdf) documents the ASIC image-buffer horizontal dot-size register at sub-CPU `$FF8062` and vertical dot-size register at `$FF8064`. This experiment sets them to 128 and 64. The VDP transfer remains main-RAM DMA, using 2,048 words per update.

**Verified by execution:** the wide scene keeps 64 scanline traces and the same 60 five-word camera packets. It expands from eight to sixteen tile columns, 128 VDP tiles, 4,096 output bytes and 4,096 bytes of main-RAM staging/DMA per update. To keep source coordinates inside the 256×256 stamp map, the wide host reference and sub-CPU use X steps `(12,8,6,4)` eighth-pixels across depth bands instead of `(16,12,8,4)` and add eight source-Y pixels before applying bank. The image therefore differs from the 64×64 scene in both area and sampling; their timing difference is not an isolated per-pixel scaling coefficient. The output sum is checked before each upload. An additive sum could still miss compensating pixel errors; no word-for-word VRAM dump was taken.

**Display check — verified by execution:** the [corrected Model 2 final screenshot](data/live-camera-wide-model2-final.png) has SHA-256 `0ca25ab992e17f38e498a29b0624b7ca498efeddc545e447a553bf47fe4a442f`. In BlastEm's 256×240 internal PNG, all 8,192 pixels of the intended 128×64 viewport are nonblack, spanning X=96–223 and Y=89–152; the road and surrounding grass are visible. This checks VDP tile-map coverage and displayed output in addition to the in-program sum, but it remains one captured frame rather than a dump of the whole animation.

**Scheduling finding — verified by execution:** the naïve loop always waited through one complete intervening VBlank after finishing work. Wide output became ready 844–870 ticks after the prior VBlank, after the first intervening edge at about 543 ticks but before the intended upload edge at about 1,086 ticks. The naïve loop then skipped the 1,086-tick edge and uploaded at about 1,629 ticks. The corrected wide-only loop checks the already recorded ready time: when it is at least 544 ticks, it waits for the next fresh VBlank without a further skip; otherwise it retains the original skip. This threshold is tied to the observed NTSC-U emulator frame period and is **not** a general hardware timing guarantee. No other build mode uses this pacing branch.

It does **not** fit the next 543-tick edge, so this result does not establish 60 Hz. The ownership figure includes setup, the ASIC and Word RAM return; the staging figure includes both checksum and copy. Main/sub-CPU cycle counts and stock-hardware durations are **unsupported**.

## Engineering decision

**Hypothesis:** 128×64 at 30 Hz is a more useful next synthetic road viewport than 64×64, given this repeated BlastEm result, but the 227-tick ready margin must be tested against scenery, more complex trace math and gameplay input. A 128×64/60 Hz target is unsupported by this schedule. The next controlled experiment should add one measured source of visual or camera work at a time while retaining the 4 KiB VDP upload and live packet interface. Physical-hardware compatibility and an arcade-faithful Power Drift engine remain unproven.
